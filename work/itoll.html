Business development

Building the case for a new automotive-services line

iToll wanted to grow beyond its core product into on-demand automotive services. As Business Line Owner on a three-month engagement, I built the business case for the new line from zero and delivered its launch plan.

Company
iToll
Role
Business Line Owner
Timeline
3 months · 2024
Deliverable
Business case & launch plan

The question

Is there a real business in on-demand automotive services for iToll, and how should it go to market?

A new business line needs more than a good idea. It needs a market that is large enough, a clear reason for customers to choose it over the alternatives, and a model that is viable for the company.

Approach

Six workstreams, from the market to the launch plan.

Exhibit 1 · Six workstreams of the business case

01

Market analysis

How big is the demand, and in which services?

02

Competitive analysis

03

Tapsi Garage (local) and AutoGuru (global): how their models work.

04

Value proposition

Why would customers choose iToll for this service?

05

Viability analysis

Can the model work for the business?

06

Launch plan

How to take the new line to market.

Deliverable: a business case and launch plan for the new lineHanded over to the team for execution

What I delivered

  • Market analysis of on-demand automotive services and where demand sits.
  • Competitive analysis and benchmark of Tapsi Garage and AutoGuru.
  • Value proposition for the new line.
  • Viability analysis of the business model.
  • Launch plan, handed over to the team for execution.

Why it matters

From a blank page to a decision-ready business case and launch plan in three months.

The engagement covered the front end of business development: reading the market, understanding the competition, defining why customers would choose the service, testing whether it works for the business, and planning how to take it to market.
