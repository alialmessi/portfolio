Business development

Launching a new on-demand automotive-services line

iToll wanted to grow beyond its core product into on-demand automotive services. As Business Line Owner on a three-month engagement, I built the business case from zero, launched the first service vertical and handed over the roadmap.

Company
iToll
Role
Business Line Owner
Timeline
3 months · 2024
Outcome
First vertical launched

The question

Is there a real business in on-demand automotive services for iToll, and if so, where should it start?

A new business line needs more than a good idea. It needs proof of demand, pricing that works, economics that hold at the unit level, and partners who can deliver the service.

Approach

Five workstreams, each answering one part of the business case.

01

Market sizing

How big is the demand, and in which services?

02

Customer research

What do drivers need, and what would they pay for?

03

Benchmarking

Tapsi Garage (local) and AutoGuru (global) service models.

04

Pricing & unit economics

Can each order make money?

05

Partnerships

Who delivers the service, and on what terms?

Decision: launch car wash as the first verticalThen expand service by service along a roadmap

What I delivered

  • Business case for the new line: market size, customer insight, pricing and unit economics.
  • Competitive benchmark of Tapsi Garage and AutoGuru to shape the service model.
  • Partnership analysis for delivering services through third-party providers.
  • Launch plan, with car wash going live as the first service vertical.
  • Roadmap handover so the team could keep adding verticals after the engagement.

Why it matters

From a blank page to a live service in three months.

The engagement covered the whole path of business development: finding the opportunity, proving it with numbers, choosing where to start, and getting it into customers’ hands.
